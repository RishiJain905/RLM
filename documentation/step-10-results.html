The 10d test found a real problem, now fixed

Resuming from an earlier checkpoint didn't work on W&B serverless: the forked branch copied checkpoint 15 correctly, but training then started from a fresh LoRA. Production's rollbacks and its 2e-5 → 1e-5 fallback would have silently restarted from the base model. The plan's KL check couldn't see it; comparing the LoRA weights did. A fresh branch model seeded with the checkpoint's LoRA does train on from it, tested live, and the loop now resumes that way (§8).

Why 83% and not ~70% (inferred)

Most of the gap looks like environment v2 itself rather than training: this seed's training gain was only +4 to +8 points, and the untrained model was already stronger at step 1 (62.5% vs 53%). Against the first seed's held-out run, the model submits more often (0.98 vs 0.88, about +8 points) and is right more often when it does (0.85 vs 0.80, about +5). Environment v2 changed the reward, the clip, the sandbox and the thinking setting at once, so this can't single one out.

To settle it: add the base model on these held-out tasks (environment v2, prompt v1) to step 11's baselines, 100 episodes for about $2. If it lands near 0.60-0.65, the seed's gain matches the first seed's +20 and the replication holds.

  • The suspicious win: the REPL cuts printed output at 2,000 characters, so the model saw 16 of a customer's 17 tickets.   • Detector precision 0 of 2: both escape_attempt hits were the model saving sub-call results to /tmp. It didn't retry and solved the task normally; one correct answer lost its reward. Prompt v1 never says where the model may write.

06W&B rate limiting

W&B returned 429 "too many requests" on 19-47% of request attempts from step 2 on (3,103 in all), even within our caps of 30 in flight and 20 per model. Step 1 had none, when every request went to one model. From step 2, root calls go to the newest checkpoint and sub-calls to the base model, and the 429s track sub-call volume (correlation 0.74), not root calls. Six retries from 2 s cover about a minute. That held until step 20, where 7 requests failed six times in a row.

Rishi's decision: spread out sub-call requests in production, even if it is slower.

07Cost and time

  • $0.67 a step ($0.60-0.78): 20 steps used $13.77 of the $14 training ceiling. Step 20 only just fit, and 10d needed the ceiling raised to $15.
  • What drives cost (inferred from tokens): root prompts 49%, root output 30%, sub-calls 21%. Each root call re-sends the transcript (6k tokens at turn 4, 54k at turn 12), and no prompt tokens were cached. A tickets_all episode costs 3.7× a tickets_customer one.
  • The eval cost $1.97 against the loop's $3.24 estimate (1.5× the training cost per episode). That margin is why the $3 ceiling stopped the run before its eval.
  • A step is rollouts (median ~180 s, falling from 317 s to 176 s over the run) plus training on W&B (~200 s, of which ~75 s is GPU time). Rollouts wait for the slowest episode.

0810d: resuming from an earlier checkpoint

train.py resume second-seed-v1 --from 15 --stop-after 16 --ceiling-train 15 --live

The loop forked checkpoint 15 into a new model, second-seed-v1-b1, trained one step on it ($0.66) and stopped. The plan's check compared step 16's KL to base: 0.0043 on the fork against 0.0031 originally. That looks fine, but it can't see the problem: step 16's rollouts are sampled from checkpoint 15, the original branch's model either way. Only the training touches the fork.

The copy is exact (distance 0 from checkpoint 15). The trained fork is small, and as far from checkpoint 15 (0.864) as checkpoint 15 is from zero: one step from a fresh LoRA. W&B also named it step1, not step16.

  • Why (inferred from ART 0.5.20's code): the fork only uploads the source checkpoint as an artifact for the new model, and its own docstring warns that serverless checkpoint tracking may not reflect it. Our loop registers the new model first, which creates its step 0, and forks second. The training service then continued from its own step 0.
  • For production at 2e-5: the learning-rate fallback and any stop-rule rollback would restart from the base model while the logs say "continuing from checkpoint N".

The fix, tested live

W&B trains each model from the newest step in its own checkpoint list, loading that step's LoRA by its artifact alias and checking it against the optimizer state saved with it. Forking before registering left the list at step 0. Deleting newer steps was refused ("Cannot delete the last checkpoint"). New weights under an existing step were loaded, then refused ("Optimizer checkpoint and loaded LoRA weights do not match"). What works: a fresh model, with only step 0 and no optimizer state, whose step0 holds the checkpoint's LoRA. Trained one step on a saved batch ($0 inference, ~$0.16 GPU):
